# Paper-Cutout Diorama Script — "Abuna Salama: Father of Peace"

## 1. CONCEPT BLOCK

- **Working title:** *Abuna Salama: The Father of Peace* — How Christianity Took Root in Africa
- **Style:** A tilt-shift documentary-diorama world — miniature paper-cutout sets shot with shallow depth of field, muted archival color grading, and cinematic 9:16 letterboxed framing with elegant serif documentary captions — fused with vintage editorial paper-collage craft: visible paper grain and fiber texture, cleanly cut character edges with soft fraying shadows, raw torn deckle-paper background edges, faint halftone newsprint dot-texture, thin strips of aged red paper tape as scene/transition accents, and subtle grid or graph-paper card backings, all lit with warm tungsten key and cool ambient fill, fine film grain, and floating dust motes. No flat vector-cartoon shading, no poster-flat colors, no glossy CGI render, no smooth 3D shading, no plastic look, no anime/cartoon facial style, no modern clothing or objects, no digital-illustration line art.
- **Format:** 9:16 vertical, thin cinematic letterbox bars top and bottom
- **Runtime:** ≈89 sec (15 story shots + 1 bonus CTA shot)
- **Main character:** Frumentius of Tyre (4th century CE), later consecrated as Abuna Salama — "Father of Peace" — the first bishop of Aksum. A real, documented historical figure: a Phoenician student shipwrecked as a boy on the Red Sea coast of the Kingdom of Aksum (in the highlands of what is now Ethiopia/Eritrea), raised at the royal court, who later traveled to Alexandria, was consecrated bishop by Patriarch Athanasius, and returned to convert King Ezana and found the church that became the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church — one of the oldest continuously practiced Christian traditions in the world. Physically: a slender, upright-postured man, cut from warm medium-brown craft cardstock with fine visible paper grain, short black curling hair, deep-set warm brown eyes, calm watchful expression with a patient half-smile; shown as a beardless youth in early shots and, from the midpoint on, as a bearded adult bishop in an indigo paper mantle appliqued with a red-paper-tape cross, wearing a small hand-cut wooden cross pendant on a braided paper cord as his one constant distinguishing detail.

## 5. AUDIO NOTES

**Music arc:** Open on a single sparse, plaintive lyre-or-oud-like plucked motif under Shots 1–3 (mystery, danger, a life upended), thinning to near-silence at the moment of shipwreck. From Shot 4 through Shot 8, layer in a slow, rising ensemble of low frame drum, a bowed string drone, and a wordless vocal hum, building patiently to mirror Frumentius's rising standing and eventual consecration. Shot 9 (the coin, the king's conversion) is the musical high point: full but still warm and acoustic instrumentation, a triumphant but restrained modal melody, never bombastic or electronic. Shots 10–12 pull back to a steadier, hymn-like processional rhythm — bell-like tones, layered vocal harmony — evoking permanence and ritual. Shot 13 swells briefly and widens the arrangement (more voices, wider stereo image) to suggest continental scale, then Shot 14 strips back down to the solo opening motif, now played on a warmer, fuller instrument, closing the emotional circle. Shot 15 resolves on a single sustained, consonant chord that fades slowly. Shot 16 introduces a brighter, contemporary but still warm/acoustic sting under the CTA, distinct enough to signal "bonus" without breaking the film's mood.

**Recurring SFX:** Paper rustle and fine grain crackle under every camera move; creaking timber and lapping water for ship/harbor scenes (Shots 2, 3, 6, 8); dry wind and distant hoofbeats for the shipwreck and raid (Shot 3); soft footsteps on woven matting and the whisper of scroll-unrolling in court and scriptorium scenes (Shots 4, 5, 10); a low ceremonial bell and soft breathing/incense-hiss for the consecration (Shot 7); a metallic coin-strike clink layered with forge crackle (Shot 9); distant monastic chanting, very faint, under Shots 11–14; a soft paper-pin "tick" sound on the CTA pointing gesture (Shot 16).

**Narrator tone:** A warm, measured, unhurried documentary voice — think reflective long-form historical narration rather than trailer-style hype. Slight intimacy and gravity in the lower register, natural pauses between clauses, a voice that sounds like it is telling you something it genuinely finds moving, never rushed even inside the six-second shot windows.
