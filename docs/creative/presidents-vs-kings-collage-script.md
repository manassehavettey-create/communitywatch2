# Paper-Collage Editorial Animation Script

## CONCEPT BLOCK

**Working title:** *Power, On Loan*

**Style:** A flat, physically-photographed paper-collage and retro-scrapbook photomontage built from cleanly cut black-and-white photographic cutouts of people, buildings, objects, and hands — with one or two areas of selective, deliberate saturated color — arranged as bold graphic compositions on richly textured, grainy aged card stock in an editorial, political-magazine layout sensibility, using cut-paper headline type, torn newspaper clippings with real headline text, numbered captions, and small credit lines, all built from naturalistic, physically-layered paper (never vector, CGI, or diorama-style rendering).

**Format:** 9:16 vertical, thin cinematic letterbox bars top and bottom.

**Runtime:** 75–90 sec (15 story shots + 1 bonus CTA shot).

**Main character:** General George Washington, grounded in the real 1782 Newburgh episode (Colonel Lewis Nicola's letter urging Washington to become king, and Washington's written refusal) and his real, precedent-setting voluntary departure from the presidency in 1797. Physically: a tall, upright man in his late fifties, weathered but dignified, rendered entirely as a black-and-white photographic paper cutout with crisp cut edges; gray-white hair pulled back into a paper-cutout queue; wearing a cut-paper photographic Continental Army coat; one distinguishing detail — a single small torn strip of saturated red paper tape draped diagonally across his chest like a sash, the one recurring color accent tied specifically to this character; expression calm and restrained rather than triumphant. Rendered as a black-and-white photo cutout with that one deliberate red accent.

## AUDIO NOTES

**Music arc across the 16 shots:** The score opens sparse and ceremonial under Shots 1–2 — a single low drone or muted brass note evoking old-world formality — then adds tense, slightly dissonant strings through Shots 3–4 as the "problem with kings" and Enlightenment doubt build. Shots 5–7 (war, the Nicola letter, Washington's refusal) bring in a restrained snare or hand-drum pulse, quiet and controlled rather than triumphant, mirroring Washington's own restraint. Shot 8's Constitutional Convention introduces a fuller, more optimistic instrumental layer — the piece's structural "gears turning" — that keeps building through Shots 9–11 as the precedent is set and spreads globally, reaching its fullest, warmest arrangement by Shot 11. Shots 12–13 pull back to a leaner, more analytical texture (a single piano or plucked-string motif) to let the contrast and accountability points land clearly. Shot 14 strips down to near-silence — just a held tone — for the emotional hands-and-thread beat. Shot 15 swells back up warmly but simply for the outro line, and Shot 16's CTA plays over a light, playful pluck or paper-tap rhythm, closing the piece on an inviting, lower-stakes note.

**Recurring SFX:** Paper rustle and fine paper-fiber crackle under nearly every cut; a distinct scissor-snip sound on hard cuts that involve a "reveal" (Shots 1, 6, 7, 8); a tape-peel-and-press sound whenever red paper tape slides in as a wipe or scene-divider (Shots 1, 5, 7, 10, 15); a soft quill-scratch sound under Shots 4, 6, and 7 during letter-writing beats; a single low drumbeat or heartbeat-adjacent thud on Shot 7's refusal and Shot 9's oath; distant crowd murmur/ambience under Shot 8's convention scene; footsteps fading on gravel under Shot 9's "walking away" panel; a soft page-turn or paper-flutter under every transition that isn't a hard cut; a light rubber-stamp thump on Shot 16's "SEE IT?" tab.

**Narrator tone:** Warm, measured, documentary narrator — think a calm public-radio historian rather than a movie-trailer voice: unhurried pacing, slight rises in warmth on Shots 9, 10, and 15, and a dry, understated delivery on Shot 7's "abhorrent" line so the humor and gravity both land without overplaying either.
