# The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower — Animation Script

## 1. CONCEPT BLOCK

- **Working title:** The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower
- **Style:** Tilt-shift documentary-diorama miniature photography fused with vintage editorial paper-collage art — handcrafted layered cardstock and corrugated cardboard sets and characters, shallow tilt-shift depth of field, muted naturalistic/archival documentary color grading, cinematic letterboxed framing, visible fine paper grain and fiber texture, cleanly cut character edges with soft fraying, intentionally raw torn deckle-paper edges on backgrounds, faint halftone newsprint dot-texture, thin strips of aged red paper tape as physical scene/transition accents, subtle grid or graph-paper texture on card backings, warm tungsten key light with cool ambient fill, naturalistic soft-edged shadows, fine film grain, and floating dust motes — with no flat vector-cartoon shading, no poster-flat colors, no glossy CGI render, no smooth 3D shading, no plastic look, no anime/cartoon facial style, no modern clothing or objects, and no digital-illustration line art.
- **Format:** Landscape 16:9, thin cinematic letterbox bars top and bottom.
- **Runtime:** 65 seconds (15 story shots + 1 bonus CTA shot).
- **Main character:** Victor Lustig — the real Austro-Hungarian-born con artist who, in Paris in 1925, posed as a French ministry official and "sold" the decaying Eiffel Tower for scrap metal to an unsuspecting dealer. Rendered as an original paper-cutout figure: mid-thirties, slim wiry build, pale ivory cardstock skin tone with fine visible paper-fiber texture, neatly slicked dark-brown paper hair parted at the center, a thin pencil mustache, calm sharp eyes, a fitted charcoal-grey pinstripe wool suit cut from layered textured cardstock, a crisp white paper shirt collar, a dark silk necktie, and a folded scarlet-red paper pocket square tucked in his breast pocket as his one distinguishing detail, wearing a composed, faintly knowing half-smile.

## 5. AUDIO NOTES

- **Music arc:** Open with a sparse, curious solo accordion or muted upright piano motif under Shots 1–3, establishing a wistful, old-world documentary tone. As Lustig hatches and executes the scheme (Shots 4–8), layer in a light rhythmic pulse — plucked strings or a soft brushed snare — building tension and playful momentum through the payment climax, where the music should peak with a brief, confident flourish. Pull back to sparse, uneasy strings under the getaway and fallout (Shots 9–10), echoing the earlier motif but in a minor key. Reintroduce the rhythmic pulse, slightly more frantic, as Lustig attempts the scam again and is exposed (Shots 11–12), then let the music thin out again into a slower, wandering variation during the second getaway and closing reflection (Shots 13–14). Resolve on a warm, full reprise of the opening motif under the outro card (Shot 15), fading to a light single-instrument sting under the bonus CTA (Shot 16).
- **Recurring SFX:** Soft paper rustle and crinkle on every character movement and camera move; a faint continuous tape-hiss/archival film hum under the whole piece; period-accurate footsteps on wood/stone for platform and hotel scenes; distant Parisian street ambience (murmuring crowd, a far-off motorcar horn) under wide exteriors; a soft wax-seal press sound in Shot 4; the crisp slide-and-rustle of banknotes in Shot 8; a steam-train hiss and distant whistle in Shot 9; a rotary telephone's mechanical click in Shot 12; a ship's low foghorn in Shot 13; a light paper-confetti flutter sound under the bonus CTA in Shot 16.
- **Narrator tone:** A warm, measured, classic documentary narrator — unhurried pacing, dry wit held just beneath the surface, delivering each line with the quiet authority of someone recounting a legendary true story rather than performing a dramatization.
